# QA: Hiring Manager & Talent Acquisition Personas

After generating the CV (Phase 5) and before logging to tracker (Phase 6), review the tailored CV from two independent personas:

1. **Hiring Manager Lens** — Does this CV make them want to interview this person?
2. **Talent Acquisition Lens** — Does this CV actually pass the ATS + get flagged for the right reasons?

This is a rapid mental exercise, not a full document review. Just a fast "would this trigger the right response from each gatekeeper?"

## Hiring Manager Perspective

The hiring manager reads CVs that have already cleared ATS, so they're scanning for:

- **Does this person understand the actual problem?** Not just the domain, but the *specific* problem this role is solving. A CV that mentions "search and discovery ranking" when the role is about "improving merchant match rates" shows real understanding. Generic domain language doesn't signal understanding.
- **Do the numbers back up the claim?** "Drove revenue growth" is weak. "Drove 40% annual revenue growth, achieved 85% client retention with 9.3 NPS" is strong. HMs scan for proof, not just claim.
- **Is this hands-on or just a manager?** If the JD emphasizes hands-on IC work, does the CV show delivery, PRDs, direct engineering collaboration? Or just "led teams"? HMs can smell the difference.
- **Scale match:** If the HM manages a team of 3 building for 100K users, and this CV is all "led 30+ person orgs and billion-user platforms," that's a red flag (overqualified, bored). If the opposite (always small/solo), it's a red flag too (not ready for the scope). Does the scale feel about right?
- **Recency:** Is the most relevant work recent? Or is the strongest proof 5+ years old? HMs want to see this person's skills *now*.

**HM red flags:**
- "Responsible for X" (passive, not ownership)
- "Helped the team achieve Y" (ambiguous role)
- Multiple generic adjectives without numbers ("deep expertise", "passionate", "excited")
- No real outcomes, just process ("managed X, collaborated on Y")
- Skills section lists 40+ things (generalist noise, not specialist signal)

**HM green flags:**
- Specific problem → specific outcome → specific number
- Most relevant role is recent (last 3-5 years)
- Hands-on work is front and center if the role is IC-heavy
- Scale matches the open role (±1 level)

## Talent Acquisition Perspective

The TA/recruiter cares about:

- **ATS clearance:** Did the CV pass keyword matching? (This is actually what you scored in Phase 3/5, but TAs also check: are the keywords *discoverable*, or buried in old work? Keywords front and center in the profile and skills = discovered. Keywords only in a 2019 role = discovered late or missed.)
- **Keyword density:** Is the CV *packed* with the JD's keywords, or does it feel generic? TAs notice when a CV reads like "Oh, this wasn't tailored to us" vs. "This person did their research."
- **Flag-ability:** Will this CV get flagged to the right hiring manager/team? Or will it land with the wrong team or get dismissed for missing a must-have? TAs check: do the must-haves show up *clearly* in the first half of the CV, or are they buried?
- **Title alignment:** Does the headline/title match what they're searching for? If they're searching "Director" and the CV says "VP" or "Principal Consultant", will it get caught by the search, or will the TA have to manually add the CV? (Real limitation: many companies' internal search only matches exact title terms, not semantic clusters.)
- **Experience ordering:** Does the most relevant experience come first? Or is the best proof buried under older less-relevant work?

**TA red flags:**
- JD keywords nowhere in the first three bullets or profile
- Title doesn't match what they're searching for
- Most relevant work is buried (chronological order that doesn't surface matching work first)
- Skills section is so generic it could apply to 100 candidates
- Gaps or unexplained role transitions

**TA green flags:**
- JD keywords in profile + top 1-3 bullets (discovered immediately)
- Title matches or blends smartly
- Chronological order, but most relevant work *within each role* rises to the top via bullet reordering
- Skills section is JD-specific, not generic

## Quick QA Checklist (before final approval)

**Hiring Manager Lens:**
- [ ] Does the profile show understanding of the *specific* problem?
- [ ] Are there 3+ specific numbers proving outcomes?
- [ ] Is hands-on IC work front-and-center if the role is IC-focused?
- [ ] Does the scale feel right for this role (not massively over/under-experienced)?
- [ ] Is the most relevant work recent?

**Talent Acquisition Lens:**
- [ ] Do JD keywords appear in the profile + top 2-3 bullets?
- [ ] Does the title clearly match what they're searching for?
- [ ] Are the most relevant bullets within each role surfaced first?
- [ ] Does the skills section read as tailored, not generic?
- [ ] Would an ATS text extractor find the keywords easily?

If both pass, move to Phase 6 (log to tracker). If either fails, flag which lens failed and rewrite that section.
