# Presentation Round Prep

Runs as **Phase 4.7**, conditional — only when the interview *process
itself* explicitly requires presenting back: a case-study round, "bring
your first-90-days plan," "walk us through your point of view on the
business," a formal panel presentation slot. This is different from
Phase 4.5's diagram pack (an optional thinking aid for the candidate's own
prep) — this phase exists because, at senior/exec level, presenting a
point of view is often not a bonus, it's the actual assessment mechanism
for the round.

**Distinguish this clearly from what's out of scope.** Building this
presentation is in scope because the interview process is asking for it.
Building follow-on engagement deliverables *after* being hired (a
workshop deck for a paid advisory engagement, a working prototype, a
formal proposal) is a different job and stays out of scope — see
`ip-diligence.md`'s closing note. The test: is this being presented *to
get the role*, or *after* getting it? Only the former belongs here.

## Trigger

- Interview stage/context explicitly says a presentation is required
  ("prepare a 20-minute presentation," "bring a 90-day plan to present,"
  "we'd like you to walk the panel through your approach").
- Ask directly if it's ambiguous whether a round is presentation-format
  or conversational — the prep is materially different (a deck needs a
  narrative arc and a clean visual story; a conversation just needs sharp
  talking points).

## Method

1. **Confirm the actual brief** — time limit, audience (who's in the
   room, pulled from `ip-research.md` §2.5's named-interviewer research),
   and whether specific topics were named (e.g. "your first 90 days," "how
   you'd approach X"). Don't guess the brief; ask if unclear — a wrong-shape
   presentation is worse than a slightly-late one.
2. **Build the narrative arc**, not a slide-by-slide feature list. A
   strong structure for a "your point of view / plan" ask:
   - **Where they are** (grounded in real Phase 1 research — the honest
     state of the business, not flattery)
   - **What that implies** (the 2-4 pillars/priorities this suggests,
     each tied to a specific researched fact — same discipline as Phase
     4's smart questions)
   - **How you'd approach it** (sequencing, first 90 days, what you'd need
     to learn from them before committing further — see the posture note
     below)
   - **What you need from them** (a short, genuine list — signals
     collaborative intent, not a solo pitch)
3. **Match posture to round-posture calibration** (`ip-question-bank.md`).
   A first- or second-round presentation should be framed as a hypothesis
   to pressure-test ("here's my read, keen to stress-test it against what
   you're actually seeing"), not a finished solution delivered with
   certainty — presenting total confidence on a plan for a business the
   candidate doesn't work at yet reads as overreach, not readiness. A
   later-round or explicitly-final presentation can be more assertive.
4. **Every claim gets the same sourcing discipline as the rest of this
   skill** — no fabricated metrics, no unverified figures presented as
   fact. Anything modeled or assumed carries the same visible disclaimer
   rule as `ip-diagrams.md`.
5. **Build the deliverable using the appropriate tool for the format
   asked for** — a slide deck uses the `pptx` skill or a slides Artifact
   type; a one-page leave-behind uses `docx`/`artifact-design`; a live
   walkthrough with visuals can reuse `ip-diagrams.md`'s diagram pack as
   supporting material rather than duplicating it. Don't default to one
   format without checking what the round actually calls for (a live
   spoken round with no slide request doesn't need a deck built at all —
   talking points and a mental structure may be enough; don't over-build).
6. **Rehearsal pass** — after the content exists, do a tight run-through:
   flag anything that runs over the stated time limit, any claim without
   a clear source, and any section where the narrative arc breaks (a
   pillar that doesn't clearly follow from the "where they are" section).

## What this phase does NOT do

It does not turn into ongoing deck iteration for a paid engagement once
an offer is made or a fractional/advisory contract begins — at that
point the user is doing real client work, not interview prep, and should
use `pptx`/`docx`/`artifact-design` directly for that work rather than
staying inside this skill's phase structure.
