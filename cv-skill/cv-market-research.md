# Market and company research (Phase 1)

Goal: build a short "success profile" that goes beyond the JD text itself,
so tailoring targets what the role actually screens for, not just what the
posting says.

## 1. JD parsing

Extract into three buckets before researching anything:
- **Must-have** — explicit requirements, usually load-bearing for the title
  match.
- **Nice-to-have** — everything phrased as "preferred" or "bonus."
- **Implicit signals** — repeated phrases, unusual specificity in one area
  (a strong signal about what's actually broken/urgent for this team), and
  anything that reads like it was written by the hiring manager rather than
  HR/recruiting boilerplate.

## 2. Company research

`web_search`:
- `"{company}" mission values culture`
- `"{company}" engineering blog` or `"{company}" product blog`
- `"{company}" recent news` (funding, leadership changes, product launches —
  anything from the last 6 months is worth surfacing)

Synthesise into: what stage the company is at, what they're optimising for
right now, and any culture/values signals worth mirroring in the profile
summary language (not copying verbatim — mirroring register).

## 3. Role benchmarking

`web_search`: `site:linkedin.com {job_title} {company}` and, if sparse,
the same query against 2-3 direct competitors. Look for: common prior
titles/companies among people who hold or held this role, typical tenure,
and any recurring background pattern (e.g. "most came from a marketplace
background" or "internal promotes only").

## 4. Salary reality check (where data supports it)

If the JD states or implies a band, or public comp data exists for the
role/level/market, note it plainly with source. Don't invent a number —
if nothing reliable turns up, say research was inconclusive rather than
guessing at a figure this user might repeat in a negotiation.

## 5. Checkpoint

Before moving to Phase 2, present a short summary:

> Based on research: {2-3 line success profile}.
> Key findings: {finding 1} / {finding 2} / {finding 3}.
> Does this match your read, or anything to adjust?

Wait for a one-line confirmation or correction — don't proceed on an
unconfirmed research read, since it drives every downstream tailoring
decision.
